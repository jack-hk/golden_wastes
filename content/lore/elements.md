> The **Elemental Wheel** is a traditional system used in alchemy. It divides physical [Forms](/lore/zohm#form) into four broad elements: **Wood, Glass, Stone** and **Metal**.

![Elemental Wheel](/uploads/elemental-wheel.png)
{width="350" align="right" mobileMinWidth="200" mobileMaxWidth="350" caption="Each element is connected to a direction and a traditional colour."}

## Wood — North

Wood is represented by **brown** and faces **North**.  

It is associated with living change: life and death, growth and decay, softness and adaptation. 

Wood is also connected to water, learning and the surface of the inhabited world, where living things grow and decay. Like them, wood changes as it ages and eventually returns to the earth. It represents gradual change, natural cycles and wisdom accumulated through experience.

It is often chosen for objects connected to healing, learning, cultivation and the natural world. Its association with water, buoyancy and adaptation also makes it common in boats, travelling equipment and objects intended to guide or protect travellers.

## Glass — East

Glass is represented by **white** and faces **East**.  

It is associated with transparency, truth, division, agility, dexterity, lightness, crystals and gemstones.  

Glass can reveal what lies behind it, but it can also distort what is seen or split light into separate colours. This connects it to truth, interpretation and division. Though fragile, it can hold a sharp edge and be shaped with great precision.  

It is often used in lenses, measuring instruments and delicate tools, as well as in objects intended to reveal hidden things, separate one substance from another or bring greater clarity to thought and perception.

## Stone — West

Stone is represented by **grey** and faces **West**.  

It is associated with heaviness, durability, hardness, endurance, longevity and physical resilience.  

Stone changes slowly and resists the passing of time. It represents stability, patience and the passive strength to bear weight, resist pressure and withstand hardship.

It is commonly used in foundations, monuments, defensive objects and tools expected to survive heavy use.  

## Metal — South

Metal is represented by **yellow** and faces **South**.  

Its colour comes from gold, which alchemists regard as the purest expression of metal. Gold’s resistance to decay, reflective surface and use in precise instruments have given it a central place in alchemy and science.

Metal is associated with strength, valour and pride. Its use in wealth, polished surfaces and finely made instruments also connects it to reflection, technology and discovery.

It is often chosen for weapons, scientific instruments, symbols of authority and objects intended to focus or strengthen alchemical effects.  

### Elemental Practice

The **Elemental Wheel** influences philosophy, craft and daily life.

A tool, trinket, weapon or piece of clothing may be made primarily from a chosen element to better suit its purpose. When making the entire object from that material is impractical, craftspeople may add a small plate, thread, inset stone or decorative encrustation instead.

A hunter might carry a stone charm for endurance. A scholar may place glass within a writing tool as a symbol of truth and clear thought. A healer may favour wood, while an alchemist might use gold or another metal to represent technology and discovery.

Most people understand the elements through tradition and symbolism, and many of their customary uses are based on inherited belief. Scholars, alchemists, hunters, builders and skilled craftspeople study the deeper relationships between material, purpose and [Zohm](/lore/zohm/).

To these experts, the substance of a [Form](/lore/zohm/#form) is never merely decorative. It can directly influence how effectively its Zohm may be directed.
